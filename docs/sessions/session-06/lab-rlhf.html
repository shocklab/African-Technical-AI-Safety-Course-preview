Week 3 • Session 6.5

Lab: reward models and over-optimisation

Train a reward model from preferences, then put a number on Goodhart with your own model

What we'll cover

You'll train a small reward model on preference pairs and check its accuracy on pairs from held-out prompts. Then use best-of-\(n\) sampling to compare proxy and gold scores as search increases, and fit a possible turnover curve. The last part compares the gold scorer's predictions on parallel English and isiZulu sentences.

Setup Colab: no GPU needed

Allow about an hour. Allow around ten minutes for computing on a standard Colab CPU runtime; downloads and runtime load can change this. The evaluation-generation cell alone took about six minutes in the 11 September 2026 check. The rest of the hour is for reading the outputs and exploring.

  1. Open the starter notebook in Colab, then File → Save a copy in Drive before you change anything.
  2. Colab warns that the notebook "was not authored by Google", as it does for anything loaded from GitHub. Choose Run anyway.
  3. Work down with Shift+Enter. You can also read the whole notebook here or download the .ipynb.

The code is written for you. Your work is in the two "Explore" cells: parameters to change, and short questions to answer.

① Appoint a gold standard

The toy reward

The notebook appoints a gold scorer, an English sentiment classifier, to define the toy task. It generates completions from GPT-2 and scores them by the positive-minus-negative logit difference.

② Train the proxy reward model

Bradley–Terry, in code

Compare completions to the same prompt, label each pair by gold, and train a small proxy reward model with \(\mathcal{L} = -\mathbb{E}[\log \sigma(r_\phi(x, y_w) - r_\phi(x, y_l))]\). Training and testing use different prompts and completion indices. The gold-versus-proxy design follows Gao, Schulman and Hilton: a fixed scorer defines the toy reward, so its disagreement with the proxy can be measured.

Measure held-out preference accuracy against the toy scorer’s labels. This is a different dataset and judgement task from published human-preference evaluations; a similar accuracy would not make the models equivalent.

③ Optimising against the proxy

Best-of-n and the KL axis

Best-of-\(n\) selects the highest-scoring response among \(n\) samples. The expression \(\log n-(n-1)/n\) gives a KL reference for idealised sampling with continuous reward ranks. This notebook resamples a finite candidate pool, so use that axis as an approximation and inspect ties and pool size.

Plot what happens to both scores as \(n\) increases. With this small candidate pool, gold may keep rising. A downturn is not required for a successful submission. The absence of one here does not show that larger searches are safe, or that a downturn must occur at some particular larger \(n\).

The notebook fits Gao’s best-of-\(n\) functional form, \(R(d)=d(\alpha-\beta d)\), to your points and computes a possible peak. Treat that peak as an extrapolation conditional on the fitted shape. Repeat the fit with different seeds and report whether the predicted peak is stable or supported by the observed range.

Change the seed and re-run. Record the predicted peak, or report that the fit has no positive peak, and compare the extrapolations. Weaken the proxy and check whether the predicted peak changes. Reduce the number of evaluation prompts and check how sensitive the curve is to the examples included.

Write down, in a sentence or two: you have a proxy and no gold, which is the real situation. What do you do to avoid running off the end of the curve, and what does it cost you?

④ Whose preferences does the judge encode?

The same sentence, two languages

The gold model is an English movie-review sentiment classifier. Compare its scores on parallel English and isiZulu news sentences from MAFAND-MT. Both inputs are truncated to 64 tokens, which can retain different amounts of content. The dataset supplies translations, not ground-truth sentiment labels.

Report prediction agreement, mean scores and confidence separately. Disagreement alone does not identify which prediction is wrong. Use labelled examples, checked translations and complete short sentences to investigate language comprehension, domain shift and truncation.

Write a short paragraph separating your observations from hypotheses about harm in deployment. Propose a controlled evaluation that could establish whether the judge makes more sentiment errors in one language.

Submit

One notebook, run top to bottom with outputs visible, containing the proxy's held-out accuracy, your proxy-versus-gold measurements and the predicted turnover from at least two seeds, your sentence or two on what to do without a gold standard, and your paragraph on the language gap. Graded on completion and correctness. Resubmission allowed.

Session 6 and Week 3 summary: what's next

RLHF uses preference comparisons to learn a scalar reward, then optimises against it. DPO uses comparisons directly. These methods can improve behaviour while retaining or amplifying errors in the feedback. The lab illustrates proxy optimisation and the limits of an extrapolated curve. Its multilingual extension asks what additional evidence would be needed to validate the scorer outside its training domain.
